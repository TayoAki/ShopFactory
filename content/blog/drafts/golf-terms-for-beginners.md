# Golf Terms for Beginners: 40 Words You'll Hear on Day One

**Golf terms** are the shorthand your group uses on the course. Par and bogey are scores, the turn is the break after nine holes, and the 19th hole is where everyone goes after.

Nobody expects you to know them yet. Knowing a few still makes the **first tee** feel friendlier.

The rest of this guide covers all 40, in **plain English**, grouped by where you'll hear them.

## What golf terms should a beginner know first?

Start with par, bogey, tee time, the turn and the 19th hole. Those five cover your score, your start time, the halfway break and where the group ends up.

Rules words, like winter rules or a gimme, can wait a round or two. Where a word comes from the Rules of Golf, we checked it against [the R&A's online Rules](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/definitions). The R&A and the USGA [update the Rules together](https://www.randa.org/en/articles/r-a-and-usga-announce-2023-rules-of-golf-update), and the current edition took effect on 1 January 2023.<!-- FACT NOTE: the brief asks for a check on usga.org. usga.org returned 403 again on 18 Nov 2026, so every rules definition here was checked on randa.org (same joint Rules). ## Scoring words

These are the words on your scorecard, and in the group chat after.

- **Par.** The target score for a hole, printed on the scorecard. Holes are usually a par 3, 4 or 5, and the course's par is the total for all 18.
- **Birdie.** One under par on a hole. A 3 on a par 4 is a birdie.
- **Eagle.** Two under par on a hole. A 3 on a par 5 is an eagle.
- **Bogey.** One over par on a hole, like a 5 on a par 4. The word is older than par. In 1890, Hugh Rotherham, secretary of Coventry Golf Club, proposed a "ground score" for each hole. At Great Yarmouth, a player in a competition was called "a regular Bogey man," and the name stuck. By 1892 the officers at Gosport's United Services Club had promoted him to "Colonel Bogey." After the USGA set par distances in 1911, Americans began using bogey for one over par, [Scottish Golf History records](https://www.scottishgolfhistory.org/origin-of-golf-terms/bogey/).
- **Double bogey.** Two over par on a hole. A 6 on a par 4. It happens to everyone.
- **Gross score.** Your total number of strokes, penalty strokes included, with no handicap applied. That's how [Rule 3.1c](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/the-rules-of-golf/rule-3) describes it.
- **Net score.** Your gross score adjusted for your handicap strokes. The same rule says this is done "so that players of differing abilities can compete in a fair way."
- **Handicap.** A number that reflects how you play, so golfers of different levels can have a fair game. The Rules of Handicapping say a Handicap Index "should represent" your demonstrated ability, and the highest Index is 54.0 ([R&A, Rule 5](https://www.randa.org/en/roh/the-rules-of-handicapping/rule-5)). A Course Handicap turns it into the number of strokes you receive on a given course ([Rule 6](https://www.randa.org/en/roh/the-rules-of-handicapping/rule-6)).
- **Stroke play.** Everyone counts every stroke for the round, and the fewest total strokes wins ([R&A definitions](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/definitions)).
- **Match play.** Head to head, hole by hole. You win a hole with fewer strokes, and you win the match once you lead by more holes than are left to play.

## Course words

Picture one hole from start to finish. You start on the tee box and finish in the hole on the green.

- **Tee box.** Where each hole starts. The Rules call it the teeing area: "the area the player must play from in starting the hole," a rectangle two club-lengths deep behind the tee markers ([R&A definitions](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/definitions)).
- **Tee.** The small peg you can set your ball on for your shot from the tee box. Under the Rules it can't be longer than four inches.
- **Fairway.** The short, mown grass between the tee box and the green, where you want your ball to land. The Rules don't define it. It sits inside what they call the general area.
- **Rough.** The longer grass beside the fairway. It's harder to hit from, and it's also part of the general area.
- **Bunker.** "A specially prepared area of sand," in the Rules' words. Most golfers also call it a sand trap.
- **Penalty area.** Any body of water on the course, such as a pond or a creek, plus any other area the course defines as one. The Rules mark them with yellow or red stakes or lines.
- **Out of bounds.** Everything outside the course's boundary edge. The Rules treat those areas as "not part of the course." You'll hear it shortened to OB.
- **Green.** The short, smooth grass around the hole. The Rules call it the putting green, an area "specially prepared for putting."
- **Flagstick.** The pole in the hole that shows players where the hole is. Most people call it the pin.
- **Front nine.** Holes 1 to 9.
- **Back nine.** Holes 10 to 18.
- **The turn.** The break between the front nine and the back nine. Plenty of groups grab a drink or a snack here before the 10th tee.
- **Cart path.** The paved path for golf carts. On "cart path only" days, carts stay on the path, often after rain.

## Range and practice words

The range is where nerves go to calm down. Three words cover it.

- **Range.** Short for driving range, the practice area where you hit balls without playing a hole.
- **Bucket.** A basket of range balls. Courses usually sell them in a few sizes. (Not the same as a bucket hat, though both help on a sunny range day.)
- **Warm-up.** A few minutes of stretches, easy swings and putts before your tee time. It makes the first swing of the day less of a shock.

## Playing words

You'll hear these once the round starts, often in the cart.

- **Tee time.** Your booked start time. Arrive early enough to check in at the pro shop and warm up.
- **Foursome.** In everyday golf talk, a group of four players. In the Rules, "Foursomes" with an s is a format where two partners play one ball and take turns hitting it. The Rules add that it's "also known as 'Alternate Shot'" ([R&A definitions](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/definitions)).
- **Scramble.** A team format for two, three or four players. Everyone tees off, the team picks one shot, and everyone plays the next shot from that spot until the ball is holed ([R&A Committee Procedures](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/committee-procedures/9)). Only the best shot counts, so it's a friendly format for a newer golfer.
- **Mulligan.** A do-over shot, usually off the first tee, that some friendly groups allow. You won't find it in the Rules of Golf, where your gross score counts every stroke. Ask your group before you take one.
- **Winter rules.** Golfer slang for preferred lies. When wet or wintry weather leaves the ground in poor shape, the course's committee can let you lift, clean and place your ball within a set distance, such as a scorecard length or one club-length, and not nearer the hole. It's generally limited to balls on the fairway, and the R&A lists it as Model Local Rule E-3 ([R&A winter rules FAQ](https://www.randa.org/en/articles/the-r-a-2024-2025-winter-rules-faq-s)). Ask the pro shop whether it's in effect that day.
- **Gimme.** A short putt your group agrees to count without you hitting it. In match play, [Rule 3.2b](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/the-rules-of-golf/rule-3) lets a player "concede the opponent's next stroke." In stroke play, "a player must hole out at each hole," so gimmes belong to match play and friendly rounds.
- **Honor (or honour).** The Rules define it as "the right of a player to play first from the teeing area." In stroke play, the player with the lowest score on the last hole has it on the next tee ([Rule 6.4b](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/the-rules-of-golf/rule-6)), so that's who hears "you've got the honor."
- **Ready golf.** Hitting when you're ready instead of waiting for strict turn order. In stroke play, Rule 6.4 says players are "both allowed and encouraged to play 'ready golf'," as long as it's safe.
- **Fore.** What you shout when your ball is heading toward someone. Shout it loud and early. If you hear it, cover your head.
- **Ball marker.** A coin, a tee or a small flat marker you set right behind your ball on the green before you lift it. The Rules list "a tee, a coin, an object made to be a ball-marker" as examples.

## Clubhouse words

These are the words for everything before the first tee and after the last putt.

- **Pro shop.** Where you check in for your tee time, pay, and buy balls, gloves and hats.
- **Clubhouse.** The main building, often with locker rooms and a bar or restaurant. Dress codes vary from club to club, as [Golf Monthly points out](https://www.golfmonthly.com/features/im-a-long-time-golf-club-member-and-the-dress-code-still-confuses-me). Whether your hat comes off indoors has [its own etiquette guide](/journal/golf-hat-etiquette).<!-- OWNER CHECK: 11.3 #8. Sibling links assume the /journal/<slug> route. -->
- **Starter.** The person who sends each group off the first tee, in order, at its tee time.
- **19th hole.** The bar or restaurant where the group goes after 18 holes. Lunch after the round, the replay of every putt, and the group photo.

## Golf terms FAQ

### Do I need to know all 40 terms before my first round?

No. Learn fore, tee time and the parts of the hole, and keep up with the group. The rest sinks in by the turn.

### What should I wear for my first round?

Check the course's dress code first, then build from there. Our guide to [what to wear golfing for the first time](/journal/what-to-wear-golfing-women) walks through it, hat included.

### What's the difference between the turn and the 19th hole?

The turn is the short break after nine holes. The 19th hole is where you go once all 18 are done.
