# Golf Hat Etiquette: When to Take Your Hat Off (and When Not To)

You don't have to wear a hat to play golf. Hats off in the clubhouse is still the custom in the beginner guides, and taking your cap off for the **handshake on the 18th green** is optional. The handshake is the part that matters.

**Golf hat etiquette** splits golfers into two camps. One says caps come off indoors and on 18, out of respect. The other calls that old-fashioned, and **hat hair** is part of its case.

We read both sides and the beginner guides. Here's where we land on each question:

## Do you have to wear a hat golfing?

No. [Glenmuir's first-round guide](https://www.glenmuir.com/news/first-golf-game-clothing-guide) says "wearing a hat is entirely optional," though "many golfers prefer to wear one."

There are good reasons to. [Ship Sticks](https://shipsticks.com/blog/golf-for-beginners-part-2-what-to-wear) says a hat or visor helps keep sweat off your eyes and helps block UV rays. The [Skin Cancer Foundation](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/sun-protection-habits-golf-course/) calls caps and visors "good options which will stay in place and provide coverage to your face."

So wear one for the sun and the sweat, not because anyone requires it. If you want a cap that looks right from the first tee to the clubhouse table, our [embroidered golf caps](/collections/embroidered) have our golfer stitched on the front.

## Should you take your hat off in the clubhouse?

Yes, unless your club says otherwise. Glenmuir is firm: "you should always take your hat off when entering the clubhouse."

Not everyone agrees with the custom. In a [Golf Monthly staff debate](https://www.golfmonthly.com/features/would-they-rather-see-my-hat-hair-and-shake-the-hand-that-was-raked-over-my-sweaty-scalp-should-golfers-remove-their-caps-on-the-18th-green) from January 2026, features editor Nick Bonfield said he had "argued the requirement to remove your hat in the clubhouse is old-fashioned and outdated." A reader whose comment prompted the piece, Mark Dupuy, put it bluntly: "I have NEVER agreed with the hat off in the clubhouse rule."

Opinions don't change your club's policy, though. Golf Monthly's Alison Root notes that "dress codes vary from club to club," in a [piece on dress-code confusion](https://www.golfmonthly.com/features/im-a-long-time-golf-club-member-and-the-dress-code-still-confuses-me). Our advice:

- Take your hat off at the clubhouse door if you're not sure.
- Check the posted dress code or ask the pro shop.
- Not sure whether your visor counts? Ask. It's a fair question.

## Do you take your hat off on the 18th green?

It's a custom, not a requirement. Five Golf Monthly writers argued it out in the same January 2026 debate, and none of them said they'd be offended either way.

- **Nick Bonfield:** "I don't think you should have to take your hat off on the 18th green if you don't want to."
- **Matt Cradock:** "For me, it is just a respect thing," though it won't bother him if someone forgets.
- **Elliott Heath:** It's "a mark of respect and just something us golfers do," but he "certainly will not get offended" by a handshake in a cap.
- **Jonny Leighfield:** "I'd be far more annoyed if someone didn't shake my hand at all."
- **Baz Plummer:** He takes his off "because that's the custom," but wouldn't be offended if you didn't.

One thing stands out. The debate is between men, and nobody mentions a ponytail pulled through the back of a cap, braids, a silk press or edges you laid that morning. Taking a cap off means something different when your hair was styled around it.

Our call? Shake hands, look her in the eye and say "good game." Take the cap off if you want to.

## Can you wear a hat backwards on a golf course?

Better not. Glenmuir warns golfers to "be careful not to wear it backwards on the course, as many golf courses don't allow this."

The driving range can be different. Glenmuir says a standalone range lets you "hit balls in whatever clothes you like." A range at a golf course, near the clubhouse, may expect you to "dress the part," so wear what you'd wear on the course.

## Are visors, beanies and bucket hats OK?

Yes, in general, though your course's dress code has the final say.

- **Visors.** Glenmuir says visors are "especially popular with female golfers," and the Skin Cancer Foundation names caps and visors as good choices on the course. If your hair is up, [the visor option](/collections/visors) leaves it free.
- **Beanies.** Ship Sticks says that "if the temperatures are colder, you may want to consider wearing a beanie instead." For winter tee times, [a cuffed beanie for frosty rounds](/products/winter-rules-beanie) like our Winter Rules Beanie has our golfer embroidered on the cuff.
- **Bucket hats.** [Druids' golf hat guide](https://www.druids.com/blogs/golf/golf-cap-vs-visor) treats the bucket hat as a normal third option next to the cap and the visor, with wider shade than a standard cap.

New to the words people use on the course, like "the turn" or "the 19th hole"? Our list of [golf terms for beginners](/journal/golf-terms-for-beginners) explains them.

## What about hat hair?

If you've ever hesitated at the clubhouse door, hat hair was probably why. The Golf Monthly writers complain about it too. Leighfield dislikes "a squashed and sweaty mop of hair," and Plummer describes "horrendous hat hair which is jutting out from your head at every possible angle."

A few practical moves:

- **Pick the hat for the day.** A visor has no crown, so nothing sits on top of your hair. A cap with a ponytail opening lets your ponytail come out the back instead of folding under the band.
- **Pack a backup.** A scarf, a headband or a clip in your bag covers the lunch-after-the-round moment.
- **Keep it on outside.** The clubhouse custom is about going indoors. On the patio, follow what the club allows.

No hat makes hat hair disappear, ours included. A visor or a ponytail opening just gives your hair more room.
