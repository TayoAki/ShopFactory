# Black Women's Golf Groups and Clubs to Join

**Black women's golf groups** give you people to play with, from Wake-Robin Golf Club, founded in 1937, to a free sisterhood with chapters in six metro areas. Below are five groups we checked on their own websites on 18 November 2026, plus one national association open to all women.

Golf is easier to stick with when someone is **waiting for you on the first tee**. Each group here was built around Black women golfers or women of color, and several welcome beginners.

## Why join a golf group?

A group gives you people to play with and tee times on the calendar. That matters, because the National Golf Foundation reports that women are "around twice as likely to become 'lapsed golfers'" ([NGF, 2026](https://www.ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Female-1-pager-2026.pdf)).

A group won't fix that on its own. It does mean someone texts you on Saturday morning to ask if you're in.

You also won't be the only new face. In 2025, 36% of the 3.3 million US beginner golfers were female, according to the same NGF report.

## Long-standing clubs

These clubs were founded by Black women when many courses and clubs shut them out, and all three still run today. Their history gets its own post: [our short history of Black women in golf](/journal/black-women-golf-history).<!-- OWNER CHECK: 11.3 #8. Sibling links assume the /journal/<slug> route. -->

### Wake-Robin Golf Club

Helen Webb Harris founded Wake-Robin on April 22, 1937. Thirteen women came to the first meeting, at her home on R Street NW in Washington, D.C. The club calls itself "the oldest registered Afro-American woman's golf club in the United States" ([Wake-Robin, About us](https://wake-robingolf.org/about-us)).

- **Where:** Founded in Washington, D.C. In 1938 it petitioned Interior Secretary Harold Ickes to desegregate the city's public courses.
- **Who it's for:** "Any amateur woman golfer as defined by the current rules and policies of the USGA handbook," per its [membership page](https://wake-robingolf.org/membership).
- **What it does:** It's a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that gives scholarships to college-bound female high school graduates.
- **How to join:** Applications are accepted year-round. Download the application from the membership page and send it in with the $25 non-refundable application fee.
- **Site:** [wake-robingolf.org](https://wake-robingolf.org/about-us)

### The Green's Ladies Golf Club

On May 1, 1954, Lorraine Sawyer invited seven fellow golfers to her West Philadelphia home, and the club began. It calls itself the "oldest African American women's golf club in the Delaware Valley" ([The Green's Ladies, About](https://greensladiesgolfclub.com/about/)) and held its first tournament in 1955.

- **Where:** Philadelphia and South Jersey.
- **Who it's for:** Women who already play. Its [membership page](https://greensladiesgolfclub.com/join/) welcomes women who play regularly, have on-course experience and know golf etiquette and basic rules. It suggests newer golfers try a local beginner program first.
- **What it does:** League play, tournaments and social outings, plus a youth and family hub on its [home page](https://greensladiesgolfclub.com/).
- **How to join:** Apply online from the membership page.
- **Site:** [greensladiesgolfclub.com](https://greensladiesgolfclub.com/)

### Ebony Ladies Golf League

Louise B. Horne founded the league on March 15, 1974, at Pipe O' Peace Golf Course in Riverdale, Illinois, with 14 women from local golf clubs. The league says it was the first African American women's golf league in the Midwest ([Ebony Ladies Golf League, About](https://www.ebonyladiesgolfleague.org/about)).

- **Where:** Chicago, playing courses and country clubs in and around the city.
- **Who it's for:** Its about page promises "golfing opportunities for women of all skill levels."
- **What it does:** League golf and an annual Jack & Jill Tournament. The 51st was set for August 2, 2026, per its [home page](https://www.ebonyladiesgolfleague.org/).
- **How to join:** Applications are open year-round on its [join page](https://www.ebonyladiesgolfleague.org/join-elgl). The league holds an annual spring meeting on the fourth Sunday in March, before the season starts.
- **Site:** [ebonyladiesgolfleague.org](https://www.ebonyladiesgolfleague.org/)

## Social clubs and clinics

These newer groups lean into lessons, clinics and community, and both welcome golfers who are just starting.

### Black Girls Golf

Tiffany Mack Fitzgerald founded Black Girls Golf in 2013. It describes itself as a 501(c)(3) "that empowers African American women to thrive on and off the golf course" ([Black Girls Golf, About](https://blackgirlsgolf.net/about)).

- **Where:** Chapters in the Atlanta Metro Area, Dallas-DFW, the Washington DC Metro Area, Houston, Charlotte and Raleigh/Durham, per its [chapters page](https://blackgirlsgolf.net/chapters/), plus an online community.
- **Who it's for:** African American women. Its membership page says no dress code or golf experience is required.
- **What it does:** Weekly 9-hole leagues and a beginner golf clinic in select cities, golf lessons, events and a Find a Coach directory.
- **How to join:** Membership is free, per its [membership page](https://blackgirlsgolf.net/membership/).
- **Site:** [blackgirlsgolf.net](https://blackgirlsgolf.net/). The older blackgirlsgolf.com address now forwards to a domain-sale page, so use the .net site.

### Women of Color Golf

Clemmie Perry established Women of Color Golf and its youth program, Girls on the Green Tee, in 2014 ([Women of Color Golf, About](https://www.womenofcolorgolf.org/about-1)). Its about page says it uses golf, mentors and the business community to help minority women and girls learn the game.

- **Where:** Tampa, Florida, per the address on its [home page](https://www.womenofcolorgolf.org/).
- **Who it's for:** Women and girls, especially from diverse communities. Girls on the Green Tee serves girls ages 10-17.
- **What it does:** Golf instruction for professional women, corporate clinics, leagues, social events and golf trips.
- **How to join:** Join the mailing list, or email the group from its [join page](https://www.womenofcolorgolf.org/join). Its paid Leaderboard membership is $1,000 a year and is aimed at major donors and sponsors.<!-- Checked on the group's join page, 18 Nov 2026. -->
- **Site:** [womenofcolorgolf.org](https://www.womenofcolorgolf.org/)

## National options open to all women

### LPGA Amateur Golf Association

The LPGA Amateur Golf Association isn't specific to Black women golfers, but it reaches a lot of cities and it welcomes beginners through its Let's Go Golf 101 program ([LPGA Amateurs, Memberships](https://lpgaamateurs.com/memberships/)).

- **Where:** Local chapters around the country. Members who don't live near a chapter can join At Large.
- **Who it's for:** Members must be 18 or older.
- **What it does:** Full members get local chapter golf, networking and social events. Both membership types include access to Let's Go Golf 101.
- **How to join:** Sign up on the memberships page. On 18 November 2026, full membership dues for new members ran from $65 to $120 by age group, and a Virtual Community membership was $49.99.<!-- Checked on lpgaamateurs.com/memberships, 18 Nov 2026. Recheck before publishing. -->
- **Site:** [lpgaamateurs.com](https://lpgaamateurs.com/memberships/)

## How to find a group near you

Start with the groups that already have chapters, then widen the search.

<!-- IMAGE: black-womens-golf-groups-us-map.svg (Path C SVG, see heroImage), placed here after the first paragraph. Width and height set explicitly, loading="lazy", decoding="async". -->

1. **Check chapter lists.** Black Girls Golf lists six chapters, and the LPGA Amateurs have local chapters in many areas.
2. **Ask at your local course.** The pro shop at a public course will know which women's leagues and clinics play there.
3. **Search your city.** Try your city's name with "Black women golf" on Instagram and in local Facebook groups. Plenty of newer groups start there.
4. **Ask four questions before you join.** Is it open to beginners? What does it cost? How often does it play? Where?
5. **Start one.** Black Girls Golf takes applications to start a chapter. A new chapter launches with an in-person meetup once it has volunteer ambassadors and at least 25 active members.

## What to bring to your first meetup

Bring what you'd bring to any first round, plus a friend if you can.

- **Clubs, if you have them.** If you don't, ask the organizer whether the clinic has clubs you can borrow.
- **Clothes that meet the course's dress code.** Our guide to [what to wear golfing for the first time](/journal/what-to-wear-golfing-women) covers outfits by weather.
- **Sunscreen.** The Skin Cancer Foundation's [golf advice](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/sun-protection-habits-golf-course/) is water-resistant, broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, reapplied every two hours or more often if you're sweating heavily. That's general guidance, not medical advice.
- **Water and a snack.** Clinics and 9-hole leagues can run longer than you expect.
- **A hat that fits your head and your hair.** We make [golf hats made for women](/collections/all) who play, with ponytail openings, a small fit and adjustable closures.
- **Your golf buddy.** Two new faces are easier than one. If you're both buying, look at [matching hats for the group](/pages/golf-buddy).

## Black women's golf groups FAQ

### Is there a Black women's golf group in Atlanta, Houston, DC, Charlotte or Dallas?

Yes. Black Girls Golf lists chapters in all five, plus Raleigh/Durham. In Washington, D.C., Wake-Robin Golf Club has been around since 1937.

### Is there a Black women's golf club in Chicago?

Yes. The Ebony Ladies Golf League has played in the Chicago area since 1974 and says it's open to women of all skill levels.

### Are there Black women's golf groups for beginners?

Yes. Black Girls Golf says no golf experience is required, and Women of Color Golf runs instruction for women. The Green's Ladies Golf Club asks for on-course experience, so it's a better fit once you've played a season.

### Is blackgirlsgolf.com still the Black Girls Golf site?

No. On 18 November 2026 that address forwarded to a domain-sale page. The group's site is blackgirlsgolf.net.
