# What Is a Rope Hat? Why Golfers Wear Them

A **rope hat** is a cap with a braided rope running across the front, just above the brim. Everything else about it is a regular cap.

The rope is a **style detail**, a nod to classic golf caps. We found no record of a job it does, and that's fine. **The look is the point.**

Here's what to know before you pick one.

## What is a rope hat?

A rope hat is a baseball-style cap with a cord braided across the front, where the crown meets the brim. Imperial, a golf headwear maker, defines it as a cap with ["a braided rope detail positioned across the front panel where the crown meets the brim."](https://imperial1916.com/blogs/field-notes/what-is-a-rope-hat-origins-styles-and-why-golfers-wear-them)

We make two [golf rope caps](/collections/rope-caps). Both are five-panel caps, and both have our golfer embroidered on the front.

## What is the rope on a hat for?

As far as we can find, the rope is decoration. We found no source that shows it does a job, such as keeping the cap on in the wind.

Imperial calls the rope a "visual and stylistic signature" that creates "separation between the crown and visor." On where it came from, Imperial's own article admits that "exact origins vary depending on the manufacturer and sport." The same article gives a decade-by-decade history but cites no sources for it, so we're not repeating it here.

So the honest answer to "what's the point of the rope?" is the look. That's a good enough reason to wear a hat.

## Are rope hats back in style?

GOLF.com said so in 2021. Its rope hat roundup said they ["have made their way back into mainstream style"](https://golf.com/lifestyle/best-rope-hats-golfers/?amp=1) after being "popular several years ago."

That same roundup didn't label any of its rope caps as a women's style. We don't have sales figures that show how big the trend is now, and we won't guess.

What we can tell you is closer to home. A rope cap is the hat that started Bogey Baby.

## Five-panel vs six-panel rope caps

A five-panel cap has one wide front panel with no seam down the middle. A six-panel cap has a seam running up the center of the front.

Imperial describes the five-panel front as a "broad, uninterrupted surface." Six-panel caps, with their "stitched panel divisions," deliver "the classic structure many golfers associate with traditional golf headwear," in Imperial's words.

The practical difference is the logo. On a five-panel cap, a centered logo sits on one piece of fabric. On a six-panel cap, a centered logo crosses the center seam.

| | Five-panel rope cap | Six-panel rope cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front | One wide panel, no center seam | Two panels joined by a center seam |
| Centered logo | Sits on one piece of fabric | Crosses the center seam |

## How to wear a rope hat, on the course and after

Wear it like any golf cap: on the course with your usual golf clothes, then at the 19th hole and lunch after. The rope and the golfer already give the cap a focal point, so a plain polo or quarter-zip lets them stand out.

A few practical notes:

- **Hair.** Neither of our rope caps has a ponytail opening. Wear your hair down, or in a low ponytail or bun that sits below the strap. For a high pony or a bun, see our guide to [golf hats with a ponytail hole](/journal/golf-hat-with-ponytail-hole).
- **Your first round.** A rope cap goes with whatever you'd wear to play. If you're still working out the rest of the outfit, here's [what to wear for your first round](/journal/what-to-wear-golfing-women).
- **Indoors.** Hat customs in the clubhouse and on the 18th green get their own guide: [when to take your hat off](/journal/golf-hat-etiquette).
