# What Does UPF 50+ Mean on a Golf Hat?

**UPF (ultraviolet protection factor)** is a rating of how much UV radiation a fabric lets through to your skin. A UPF 50 fabric blocks 98 percent of the sun's rays, according to the [Skin Cancer Foundation](https://www.skincancer.org/prevention/sun-protection/clothing/clothing-umbrella-sun-protection/), and UPF 50+ means a rating of 50 or higher.

On a golf hat, the number describes **the fabric itself**. It tells you nothing about the parts of you the hat doesn't cover, and those parts can still burn.

## What does UPF mean?

UPF stands for ultraviolet protection factor. The Skin Cancer Foundation says it "indicates how much UV radiation (both UVB and UVA) a fabric allows to reach your skin." The higher the number, the less UV gets through.

So is a UPF hat different from a normal cap, or is it a label? The label is a measurement. A normal cap's fabric has no number at all, and everyday fabric can be low. The Skin Cancer Foundation puts a white T-shirt at "a UPF of about 7," and a wet one at a UPF of only 3.

The foundation lists what changes a fabric's protection:

- **Weave.** Tightly woven cloth protects more than sheer, thin or loosely woven cloth.
- **Color.** Dark or bright colors absorb UV instead of letting it through.
- **Fiber.** "Shiny polyesters and even lightweight satiny silks can be highly protective because they reflect radiation."
- **Treatments.** Fabrics treated with UV absorbers or dyes keep out some extra UV.
- **Wet or stretched fabric.** Either one loses some of its protective ability.

## What do UPF 30, 50 and 50+ mean?

UPF 30 to 49 is "very good" protection and UPF 50+ is "excellent," in the Skin Cancer Foundation's words. At UPF 50, a fabric "blocks 98 percent of the sun's rays and allows two percent (1/50th) to penetrate."

| --- | --- |
| 30-49 | Very good protection |
| 50+ | Excellent protection |

## What's the difference between UPF and SPF?

UPF rates fabric. SPF rates sunscreen. The [Skin Cancer Foundation](https://www.skincancer.org/prevention/sun-protection/clothing/clothing-umbrella-sun-protection/) puts it this way: "UPF measures both UVB and UVA rays, while SPF measures only UVB." SPF is based on how long it takes UV-exposed skin to redden.

SPF is not a timer, though. The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/center-drug-evaluation-and-research-cder/sun-protection-factor-spf) says "SPF does not inform consumers about the time that can be spent in the sun without getting sunburn." To cover UVA as well as UVB, the FDA's [sun safety tips](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/tips-stay-safe-sun-sunscreen-sunglasses) point to broad-spectrum sunscreen, which protects against both.

On the course you want both numbers working. The Skin Cancer Foundation's [golf advice](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/sun-protection-habits-golf-course/) is a water-resistant, broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, at least one ounce, 30 minutes before you head out, reapplied every two hours or more often if you're sweating heavily. Clothing has one edge over sunscreen. The foundation's clothing guide points out that you never need to reapply it.

## Does a UPF hat protect your whole face?

No. A UPF rating covers the fabric, and only the skin under that fabric. A cap shades your crown, forehead and eyes, while [Druids](https://www.druids.com/blogs/golf/golf-cap-vs-visor) notes "your ears, the sides of your face and the back of your neck may still be exposed."

The Skin Cancer Foundation recommends "a brim of at least three inches to shade the face, scalp, neck, shoulders and upper back," and flags the tops of the ears and the back of the neck as places people overlook. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/skin-cancer/sun-safety/index.html) says that if you wear a baseball cap, you should cover your ears and the back of your neck with clothing, use sunscreen, or stay in the shade.

Openings matter too. The UPF 50+ rating on our hats describes their fabric. It doesn't cover the open back panel on the Back Nine Ponytail Cap, and it doesn't apply to the mesh backs on our other caps.

For your scalp, though, a rated hat is a strong choice. Dermatologist Dendy Engelman, MD, told the [Skin Cancer Foundation](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/ask-the-expert-how-can-i-protect-my-scalp-better/) that a hat labeled UPF 50+ "is probably the best protection for your scalp."

## Which hat styles cover the most?

The brim decides coverage as much as the fabric does. Here's what each style covers:

- **A wide brim all the way round.** The CDC says this shades your face, ears and the back of your neck. The Skin Cancer Foundation's [golf advice](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/sun-protection-habits-golf-course/) admits it "isn't the most practical option on a golf course." We don't make one.
- **A bucket hat.** It covers more of the face, ears and neck than a standard cap, Druids says, though it can feel warmer than a visor.
- **A cap.** It covers your crown and shades your forehead and eyes.
- **A visor.** It shades your face and eyes and leaves your scalp open. Druids warns that "the hairline and parting can still burn."

## FAQ

### Does a UPF hat replace sunscreen?

No. The Skin Cancer Foundation calls hats a complement to "UV-filtering sunglasses and broad-spectrum sunscreen." Your ears, neck and face still need sunscreen.

### Does a visor have a UPF rating?

Ours doesn't. The Clubhouse Visor has no UPF rating, and with no crown, any visor leaves your scalp open whatever its fabric.

### Does UPF wash out of a hat?

We haven't found an authoritative source that gives a number of washes, so we don't promise one. The Skin Cancer Foundation does say fabric loses some protection when it's wet or stretched.

### Is UPF 50 the same as SPF 50?

No. UPF rates how much UV gets through a fabric. SPF, in the FDA's definition, measures how much UV it takes to burn skin protected by sunscreen.
