# Black Women in Golf: A Short History From 1937 to Today

**Althea Gibson** was the first Black woman on the LPGA Tour. She joined in 1963, already a two-time Wimbledon champion, and Renee Powell followed in 1967.

The story of **Black women in golf** starts earlier, in clubs Black women founded, beginning with Wake-Robin in 1937. Another club got Ann Gregory into **USGA championships** in 1956.

## Who was the first Black woman on the LPGA Tour?

Althea Gibson. The LPGA's history page says the tour "broke the color barrier in 1963, when two-time Wimbledon champion Althea Gibson became the first African American to join the LPGA Tour" ([LPGA, Our History](https://www.lpga.com/75/our-history)).

## 1937: Wake-Robin Golf Club

On April 22, 1937, Helen Webb Harris hosted the first meeting of Wake-Robin Golf Club at her home on R Street NW in Washington, D.C. Thirteen women came ([Wake-Robin, About us](https://wake-robingolf.org/about-us)). Harris was an educator and a playwright as well as a golfer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Webb_Harris)).

The club calls itself "the oldest registered Afro-American woman's golf club in the United States."<!-- FACT NOTE: Wikipedia's Helen Webb Harris entry cites a 1936-dated reference; this post uses the club's own date, April 22, 1937. -->

Wake-Robin also pushed for access. In 1938, it petitioned Interior Secretary Harold Ickes to desegregate the public golf courses of the District of Columbia. Langston Golf Course opened near Spingarn High School in Northeast Washington in 1939. In 1941, the club's history says, Ickes issued an order opening public golf courses to all.

Today Wake-Robin is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. It gives scholarships to young women graduating from high school and heading to college.

## 1954: The Green's Ladies Golf Club

On May 1, 1954, Lorraine Sawyer invited seven fellow golfers to her home in West Philadelphia. With Lillian Cherry, Gladys Gray, Alma Fay Horn, Opal C. Shields, Mary Frances Simpson, Lubertha Slocum and Ann Wilson, she founded The Green's Ladies Golf Club ([The Green's Ladies, About](https://greensladiesgolfclub.com/about/)).

The eight founders wanted a welcoming place for other Black women who wanted to play. The club held its first tournament in 1955, and it still runs tournaments and outings today ([The Green's Ladies](https://greensladiesgolfclub.com/)). It calls itself the "oldest African American women's golf club in the Delaware Valley."

## 1956: Ann Gregory at the U.S. Women's Amateur

Ann Gregory was born in Aberdeen, Mississippi, in 1912 and moved to Gary, Indiana, in 1938. She took up golf in 1943 and joined the all-Black Chicago Women's Golf Association ([Indiana Golf Hall of Fame, 2016](https://indianagolf.org/indiana-golf-hall-of-fame-inducts-class-of-2016/)).

Her husband got her interested in the game, but watching LPGA co-founders Patty Berg and Betty Jameson play a match in 1945 turned her toward tournament golf. She won five United Golf Association national championships between 1950 and 1970. The Black press called her "The Queen of Negro Women's Golf" ([LPGA, 2023](https://lpga.com/news/2023/remembering-the-queen-ann-gregory)).

In 1956, her association became the first Black organization to join the USGA. That made its members eligible for USGA championships, and Gregory entered them that same year.

Both sources agree that in 1956 she became the first Black woman to compete in a USGA championship. They differ on which event came first:

- **The Indiana Golf Hall of Fame** says it was the U.S. Women's Open at Northland Country Club in Duluth, Minnesota, in July 1956, followed by the U.S. Women's Amateur later that year.
- **The LPGA's history piece** credits the U.S. Women's Amateur at Meridian Hills Country Club in Indianapolis, where she lost her first-round match to Carolyn Cudone, 2-1.

She kept coming back. From 1956 through 1988 she played in 27 USGA championships, and she finished runner-up at the 1971 U.S. Senior Women's Amateur. At 76, she won gold at the 1989 National Senior Olympics.

The barriers didn't vanish once she was in the field. At the 1959 U.S. Women's Amateur at Congressional Country Club, the event barred her from the players' dinner. Her answer, as the LPGA reports it: "I didn't feel bad. They were letting me play golf, so I got me a hamburger and went to bed." At the 1963 Amateur, another player mistook her for a maid and asked her to find coat hangers in the locker room. Gregory handed them over, and the two later laughed about it.

Back home in Gary, she helped desegregate the city's 18-hole public course in the early 1960s. She died in 1990, at 77.

## 1963: Althea Gibson joins the LPGA Tour

Althea Gibson came to golf as one of the most famous athletes in the world. She won the French Championships in 1956, then Wimbledon and the U.S. Championships in 1957 and 1958. She was the first African American to win either of those last two titles ([LPGA, 2020](https://www.lpga.com/news/2020/2020-pioneer-althea-gibson-an-almost-forgotten-figure-in-two-sports)). She also sang, with a recording contract at Dot Records and two appearances on the Ed Sullivan Show.

She made her first LPGA Tour start in 1963, at 36, just two years after the PGA of America dropped the "Caucasian Only" clause from its bylaws. From 1963 through 1977 she made 171 LPGA Tour starts, the LPGA's historian writes.

Golf didn't always let her in the front door. At one event, she had to change her shoes in the parking lot because the club didn't invite her into the clubhouse. The other players changed in their cars too, in solidarity.

In 1980, at 53, she went back to LPGA Q School. She didn't advance.<!-- FACT NOTE: Wikipedia lists a best finish of T2 after a three-way playoff at the 1970 Len Immke Buick Open. Left out until a primary source (LPGA, USGA, World Golf Hall of Fame) confirms it. -->

## 1967: Renee Powell

Renee Powell grew up on her family's golf course. Her parents built Clearview Golf Course in East Canton, Ohio, in 1948, she writes in [her own words for the LPGA](https://www.lpga.com/news/2020/2020-renee-powell-i-am-my-parents-daughter). Today the course sits on the National Register of Historic Places.

Before turning pro, she played the U.S. Girls' Junior and the U.S. Women's Amateur. She played college golf at Ohio University and Ohio State, and she qualified for the U.S. Women's Open.

She joined the LPGA Tour in 1967 as "only the second African American ever to play as a member of the LPGA Tour." She played until her final event in 1981. The players welcomed her, she writes. "The problems came from outsiders."<!-- FACT NOTE: Wikipedia gives 1980 as her last tour year; Powell's own LPGA column says her final event was in 1981. This post uses her account. -->

After the tour, she worked as a golf professional at a private club in the United Kingdom. Seven years later she came back to the US, and in time she took over her father's role at Clearview. The LPGA's 2020 note lists her as its head golf professional.

St Andrews took notice. In 2008 she became the first female golfer, and only the third American, to receive an honorary doctorate from the University of St Andrews. In 2015, when the R&A opened its membership to women, she was among the first seven invited to join.

## Black women in golf today

More women play golf on courses than ever. The National Golf Foundation counted a record 8.1 million female on-course golfers in the US in 2025. People of color make up 30% of female golfers, compared with 26% of all golfers ([NGF, 2026](https://www.ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Female-1-pager-2026.pdf)). The NGF doesn't report a figure for Black women alone, so we won't guess at one.

The clubs are still here too. Wake-Robin and The Green's Ladies still play, and newer groups run clinics and leagues in several cities. ## Black women in golf FAQ

### Who was the first African American woman to play in a USGA championship?

Ann Gregory, in 1956. The Indiana Golf Hall of Fame dates it to that July's U.S. Women's Open, and the LPGA to that year's U.S. Women's Amateur.

### Who was the second Black woman on the LPGA Tour?

Renee Powell, who joined in 1967 and played until 1981.

### What is the oldest Black women's golf club in the US?

Wake-Robin Golf Club, founded in Washington, D.C., in 1937, calls itself "the oldest registered Afro-American woman's golf club in the United States."
