# How to Wear a Golf Hat With Braids, Locs or Natural Hair

To wear a **golf hat with braids or locs**, choose by **where your hair sits**. Low, flat styles go under most caps. A high bun or puff needs an open back or a visor. Long braids or locs worn down suit a relaxed, unstructured cap or a bucket hat.

You did your hair for the week. **Your hat should work with it**, from the first tee to lunch after.

In the sections below, you'll learn:

Here's how it works.

## Which hat shape works with which style?

Match the hat to where your hair sits and how much of it goes under the crown. The less hair under the crown, the more caps will work.

Two things decide most of this table. A cap's crown sits on whatever is under it, so volume on top pushes the cap up. A visor has no crown, so it doesn't care.

## How do you wear a cap over braids or locs worn down?

Start with an unstructured, low-profile cap and loosen the closure before you put it on. Let your braids or locs fall from under the back edge, then tighten the strap until the cap feels steady.

A few details make the difference:

1. **Open the closure first.** Set the cap on your head with the strap fully open, then take it in. You avoid dragging the back band over your hair.
2. **Let the hair fall below the band.** Braids or locs worn down hang from under the back of the cap and through the gap above the strap.
3. **Keep the crown soft.** An unstructured front settles onto your head. A structured front holds its own shape, so hair underneath lifts it.
4. **Mind the profile.** A low-profile cap has a shorter crown, so less cap stands above your hair.

## Can you wear a visor with natural hair?

Yes. A visor sits on a band around your head with no crown, so nothing presses on your hair. The trade-off is sun on your scalp, along your parts and hairline.

[Druids' cap vs visor guide](https://www.druids.com/blogs/golf/golf-cap-vs-visor) says golfers with thick hair may find visors especially comfortable. In its words, "the open top prevents their hair from being pressed down inside a cap." The same guide warns that "the hairline and parting can still burn."

Hair does block some sun. A dermatologist in the [Skin Cancer Foundation's Ask the Expert column](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/ask-the-expert-how-can-i-protect-my-scalp-better/) says hair "does help shield the scalp" from some UV rays. You still need more protection, she says, "especially where you part your hair." Her advice is to apply sunscreen when your hair is dry, to wherever your hair parts and all around the hairline. With braids or twists, that means the parts between sections too. This is general guidance, not medical advice.

Sometimes. A bucket hat shades your face, ears and neck with a brim all the way round. Neither of ours has a back opening, though, so all of your hair has to fit under the crown.

Druids notes a bucket hat "can provide wider shade around the face, ears and neck," and that "the extra material may feel warmer than a visor."

## What to check before you buy: crown height, structure, closure

Three things decide how a cap sits over your hair: how tall the crown is, how stiff the front is, and how the closure adjusts.

- **Crown height (profile).** Low profile sits closest to your head. Mid and high profiles stand taller.
- **Structure.** Unstructured fronts are soft. Structured and semi-structured fronts keep their shape.
- **Closure.** Tri-glides, buckles and hook-and-loop straps stop at any point. Snapbacks click into set holes.

## FAQ

### Will a cap crush my twist-out?

Any cap with a crown sits on the hair under it, so expect some flattening where the crown touches. Druids makes the same point in reverse. A visor's open top keeps hair from being pressed down inside a cap. To keep anything from pressing on your twist-out, wear a visor and put sunscreen on your parts.
