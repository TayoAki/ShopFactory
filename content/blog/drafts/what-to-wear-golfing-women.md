# What to Wear Golfing for the First Time: A Women's Guide

For your **first round of golf**, wear a polo or golf top, golf shorts, a skirt, skort or pants, comfortable shoes with grip, a hat and sunscreen. Check the course's **dress code** first, because it changes from club to club.

That's the whole list. You don't need to look like a tour pro, and you won't be the only new face. In 2025, 36% of the 3.3 million US beginner golfers were female, roughly 7 in 10 of them adults, according to the [National Golf Foundation](https://www.ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Female-1-pager-2026.pdf).

## What should a woman wear golfing for the first time?

Wear a golf top, golf shorts, a skirt, skort or pants, shoes with grip, and a hat, and bring sunscreen. Most of it may already be in your closet.

Your first-round checklist:

- **A golf top.** A polo, a sleeveless top with a collar, or a sleeved top without one.
- **Golf bottoms.** Golf shorts, a skirt or skort, or golf pants. No denim.
- **Shoes with grip.** Golf shoes if you have them. Clean canvas shoes or sneakers work at many courses.
- **A cap or visor.** It keeps sun and sweat off your face. We sell [golf hats made for women](/collections/all), so we're biased, but you'll be glad of one by the 5th hole.
- **Sunscreen.** SPF 30 or higher, on before you leave.
- **A light layer.** A quarter-zip or pullover for a cool start.

Each item gets its own section below, with what the dress-code guides say about it.

## How do I check a course's dress code?

Look on the course website first, then call the pro shop if it isn't there. [Ship Sticks' beginner guide](https://shipsticks.com/blog/golf-for-beginners-part-2-what-to-wear) says the dress code "is typically located directly on the course website."

Checking matters because the rules aren't the same everywhere. Writing in [Golf Monthly](https://www.golfmonthly.com/features/im-a-long-time-golf-club-member-and-the-dress-code-still-confuses-me), long-time club member Alison Root puts it plainly: "dress codes vary from club to club, which is where the confusion comes in." The headline says it all. She's a long-time member, and the dress code still confuses her.

In the same piece, content creator Mia Baker recalls panicking before her first round. Could she wear leggings? Did she need a collared shirt? She didn't own golf shoes. In Root's words, "as it turned out, none of those things were actually required."

So check before you shop. Three easy ways:

1. **The course website.** Look for a page called "dress code", "attire" or "course policies".
2. **The pro shop.** Call and ask. It's a normal question.
3. **The person who invited you.** If it's a work outing or a friend's club, they'll know.

Going to a driving range first? [Glenmuir's first-round guide](https://www.glenmuir.com/news/first-golf-game-clothing-guide) says a standalone range lets you "hit balls in whatever clothes you like." If the range belongs to a golf course, near the clubhouse, it's "best to stick with the attire you would wear on the actual golf course."

## Tops

A polo is the safe pick. Ship Sticks calls a collared shirt like a polo "a classic choice that provides a neat and appropriate appearance."

Glenmuir's guidance for women is a little more flexible: "a sleeveless collared shirt or one with sleeves but no collar." So a sleeveless top is fine if it has a collar, and a sleeved golf top is fine without one.

A few things to know:

- **Tuck it in if the course asks.** Glenmuir notes that many golf courses want shirts tucked in before you step on the green.
- **Hot day?** Ship Sticks suggests lighter colors and moisture-wicking fabric.
- **Cool morning?** Glenmuir says sweaters and jackets are acceptable, and suggests a layer "that doesn't interrupt your swing," such as a pullover or quarter-zip.

The test that matters most is your own. Can you swing in it without tugging at it?

## Shorts, skorts, skirts or pants

Golf shorts, a skirt or skort, or golf pants all work. Ship Sticks says that for female golfers, "skirts, shorts, or pants can be worn at the course."

Length is where courses differ. Glenmuir says women's golf shorts "can be a few inches above the knee," but "they still cannot be short shorts as most golf courses will not allow this." Ship Sticks adds that some courses have length restrictions, so "confirm this before purchasing your bottoms."

What to check before you buy:

- **Length.** Look for your course's rule on the website, or ask.
- **Fabric.** Denim is out at most courses. Ship Sticks says "denim is not permitted at most courses."
- **Pockets.** You'll want somewhere to keep tees and a ball marker (the small flat disc you set down to mark your ball on the green).

Leggings depend on the club. They were one of Mia Baker's first-round worries, and there's no single rule for them. Check your course's dress code.

## Do I need golf shoes?

No, not for your first round at most courses. Glenmuir says that for women, "canvas shoes are acceptable," and that "most clubs and golf courses won't require golf shoes."

Ship Sticks agrees you can get away with tennis shoes at a public course if you're in a pinch. It also explains the upgrade: golf shoes "have small spikes on the bottom so they grip the grass better than a pair of sneakers."

If you decide to buy a pair later, Glenmuir recommends "soft spike or spikeless golf shoes." Until then, wear clean, comfortable shoes you can walk in for a few hours.

## Do I need a hat for golf?

You don't need one, but you'll probably want one. Glenmuir says "wearing a hat is entirely optional," yet many golfers wear one, and Ship Sticks explains why. A hat or visor helps keep sweat out of your eyes and helps block UV rays.

Sun is the bigger reason. The Skin Cancer Foundation's [golf advice](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/sun-protection-habits-golf-course/) calls caps and visors "good options which will stay in place and provide coverage to your face." Your ears and neck still need sunscreen.

Two hat customs to know for your first round, both from Glenmuir:

- Take your hat off when you walk into the clubhouse.
- Don't wear a cap backwards on the course. Many courses don't allow it.

## What to bring in your bag

Bring a glove, sunscreen, water and a layer. Here's the full list:

- **A golf glove.** Ship Sticks suggests packing "a few extra gloves in case yours get too wet to use."
- **Sunscreen.** The Skin Cancer Foundation's golf advice calls for a water-resistant, broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, at least one ounce, 30 minutes before you head out. Reapply every two hours, or more often if you're sweating heavily.
- **Sunscreen for your part.** Dermatologist Dendy Engelman, MD, told the [Skin Cancer Foundation](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/ask-the-expert-how-can-i-protect-my-scalp-better/) to apply it "when hair is dry to wherever your hair parts."
- **Sunglasses.** The Skin Cancer Foundation suggests a pair that blocks "99-100 percent of UV radiation."
- **Water and a snack.** You'll be out for a while.
- **A layer.** Glenmuir says "layers tend to be very much needed," and a quarter-zip folds small.
- **Rain gear, if the forecast says so.** Ship Sticks suggests a golf umbrella and a rain jacket.
- **A hair tie and a scarf.** For the walk from the 18th green to lunch after the round.

## What not to wear

Leave the denim, gym clothes and very short shorts at home. These are the pieces the guides flag most:

- **Jeans.** Glenmuir calls "jeans and athletic-style trousers" a "massive no-no," and Ship Sticks says denim is not permitted at most courses.
- **A plain tee and jeans.** Ship Sticks says "showing up in jeans and a t-shirt won't cut it."
- **Gym wear.** Glenmuir lists "sweatpants, running shorts, and drawstring exercise shorts" as off-limits.
- **Short shorts.** Most golf courses won't allow them, Glenmuir says.
- **A backwards cap.** Many courses don't allow it, Glenmuir notes.

Dress codes vary from club to club, as Golf Monthly points out. When you're not sure, the course's own dress code wins.

- A sleeveless top with a collar, in a light color
- Golf shorts or a skort, at a length your course allows
- Shoes with grip
- A visor, with sunscreen on your part and hairline
